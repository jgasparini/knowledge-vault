## What it is

A pre-mortem flips a normal risk review on its head. Instead of asking "what could go wrong?", the team imagines the project has already failed and works backward to explain why. Naming a specific failure up front — not a vague "what are the risks" prompt — gives people cover to say things they'd otherwise keep to themselves.

## Why it matters

Status reviews tend to surface the risks everyone already agreed to track. A pre-mortem catches the ones nobody wrote down: unstated assumptions, a schedule that only works if nothing goes wrong. Skip the exercise and those risks stay invisible until they show up as the actual failure.

## Evidence and examples

[[pre-mortem-technique]] describes running it early, before a plan is finalized, with each person writing their own reasons for the imagined failure independently before anyone speaks out loud — the independent-writing step is what stops the first answer from anchoring everyone else's guesses. The same source frames it as a periodic exercise at major milestones, not a substitute for an ongoing risk register.
