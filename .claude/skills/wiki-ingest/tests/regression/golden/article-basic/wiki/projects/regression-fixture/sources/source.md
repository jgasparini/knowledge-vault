# The Pre-Mortem Technique

A pre-mortem is a planning exercise where a team imagines that a project
has already failed, then works backward to figure out what could have
caused that failure. Naming a specific failure up front gives people
permission to voice doubts they'd otherwise keep to themselves in a
normal risk-assessment meeting.

The exercise works best early, before a plan is finalized. Each person
writes down their own reasons for the imagined failure independently
before anyone shares out loud — the independent-writing step matters,
because it stops the first answer from anchoring everyone else's guesses.

Teams that run pre-mortems report catching risks that standard status
reviews miss, particularly risks tied to unstated assumptions or overly
optimistic scheduling. The technique doesn't replace ongoing risk
tracking — it works best as a one-time exercise repeated at major
project milestones, not a substitute for a live risk register.
